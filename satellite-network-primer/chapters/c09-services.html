Section 2.5 gives each +Grid satellite two intra-plane and two inter-plane neighbors. Section 3.5 shows that serving satellites are reassigned on a 15-second schedule.

Orbital motion changes a satellite cache’s users. StarCDN distributes objects across nearby satellites through consistent hashing, trading a larger shared cache for ISL routing delay.

The second mechanism, relayed fetch, uses orbital motion to predict where content is cached. On a cache miss, a satellite first asks the nearest inter-orbit neighbor with the same bucket. That neighbor recently covered the same region and already holds its users’ requested content. Content therefore moves against orbital motion to follow the users. The authors use only inter-orbit links for this because intra-orbit ISL latency is higher in their geometry.

They report that proactive prefetching was rejected as less efficient [62, §3.3, Tab. 1]. Failure handling has two tiers. A transient failure sends the miss to the ground. A long-term failure remaps the bucket to the next available satellite [62, §3.4]. Figure 9.2 draws both mechanisms on a +Grid.

第二個機制是中繼擷取（relayed fetch），利用軌道運動預測內容所在。遇到快取遺漏時，衛星先向持有相同桶的最近跨軌道面鄰居查詢，那顆衛星片刻之前才覆蓋過同一批使用者的區域，因此已經持有這些使用者要過的內容，於是內容沿著與衛星運動相反的方向流動，跟隨使用者的需求。作者只用跨軌道面鏈路做這件事，因為在他們的幾何中同軌道面 ISL 的延遲較高，他們也報告主動預取因效率較差而被捨棄[62, §3.3, Tab. 1]。故障分兩層處理：暫時性故障變成一次向地面的快取遺漏，長期故障則把桶重新對應到下一顆可用的衛星[62, §3.4]。圖 9.2 把兩個機制畫在 +Grid 上。

The primer’s pooling model uses independent Zipf(α) request popularity. Its equal-sized objects are 21 MB, matching the StarCDN trace mean. A cache holding C objects has Che-approximated LRU hit rate \(h=\sum_i p_i(1-e^{-p_i t_C})\), with \(t_C\) solved from \(\sum_i(1-e^{-p_i t_C})=C\). Consistent hashing pools N satellites into a cache N times larger. The model isolates pooling and excludes orbital motion, relayed fetch, temporal locality and routing latency. The reference is StarCDN’s measured 60% → 71% at 50 GB and N = 4 on an Akamai trace [62].

One round downloads the global model and uploads its update, taking \(2M/R\) in transfer time. The model assumes k evenly spaced ground contacts per orbit. Each lasts at most 7 min, matching OrbitalBrain’s reported Planet passes. Each required pass adds an inter-contact wait of \(T/k\). Exchanges span multiple passes when \(2M/R\) exceeds 7 min. At k = 0, aggregation uses an always-visible intra-plane ISL ring, giving zero wait.

Total time is rounds × (transfer + waiting). Local training, station contention, acquisition delay and failures are outside the model. FedSpace’s external reference ticks are 45.8 days for synchronous training and 2.7 days for its scheduler [91]. AsyncFLEO supplies 72 h and 3 h 20 min [92, Tab. II].
